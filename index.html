Does the detail
know its parent?

We take a cabbage leaf apart into a coarse field and a finer Sihti-style residue. From the top portion only, we learn a library of how fine patches sit within coarse shapes. Can that library regrow the unseen bottom—and does the result fail when we lie about its parent?

What is and is not learned

The model stores pairs of coarse-neighborhood descriptors and corresponding 9×9 fine-residue patches. On a new location, it retrieves four similar training patches and chooses one reproducibly from the seed. Overlapping patches are blended. All three generated variants use the same patch dictionary and their residual RMS is matched to the training detail.

It does not learn an arbitrary inverse operator, a neural attention head, leaf anatomy, or an infinite-coordinate generator. It does not learn the precise phase of unseen random detail. It is a small, falsifiable test of whether conditioning on coarse structure helps a new residue land in the right place. The built-in scene deliberately contains a known parent/detail relationship; success on it is a calibration, not evidence for natural cabbages. A real photograph may fail.

Everything runs in your browser. Uploaded photos are downsampled and converted to grayscale locally; nothing is sent anywhere. Source code: CabbageSuper on GitHub. The green tint is for display only.
